HOW DOES HIV SPREAD

HIV infection is caused by Human immunodeficiency virus which is a retrovirus and over time leads to acquired immunodeficiency syndrome (AIDS) (Hattaf et al, 2018).

HIV is transmitted from one person to another through blood or body fluids. Individuals can be infected with HIV by sexual contact, needle sharing, blood transfusions, or maternal transmissions as a blood-borne virus or through breastfeeding. HIV takes eight to eleven years to progress to AIDS, which refers to the incubation period (Yashimura, K. 2017).

HOW DOES HIV PROGRESS TO AIDS

Aids is a deadly human viral infection disease caused by HIV infection. Before the discovery of the Antiretroviral therapy this disease claimed a lot of lives. The discovery of High Active Antiretroviral Therapy (HAART) led to the increase of life expectancy of the HIV patients. This type of HIV therapeutic is incurable (Lu et al, 2018)

BENEFITS OF THE ANTIRETROVIRAL THERAPY

Antiretroviral treatment (ART) enhances the health of individuals with human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) infection and also reduces transmission to other discordant couples (Makhema et al, 2019).

During the initiation of antiretroviral therapy, this helps to limit the viral reservoir, sustain the immune system, and reduce the chances of systemic inflammation. The society also benefits because there is reduced transmission of the virus, leading to reduced HIV incidence (De Clercq et al, 2022).

Moreover, it has been stated that starting ART in the initial phase of infection helps the preservation of HIV-specific immune responses by curbing the early destruction of CD4+ T cells and sustaining the ability to control viral replication(Herout et al, 2016)

STRATEGIES TO STAY ON TRACK

Set Reminders

Set reminders on your phone or use a pill organizer to stay consistent with your medication schedule.

Link to Routines

Link taking your medication to daily routines, like brushing your teeth or having breakfast.

Keep a Log

Maintain a medication log to track your doses and ensure you never miss one.

Consult Your Doctor

Talk to your doctor about simplifying your medication schedule for better adherence.

Join a Support Group

Join a support group to stay motivated and share experiences with others.

MEDICAL NON ADHERENCE

The other terms referring to adherence are dedication, observance and commitment. In the health settings adherence refers to the behaviour in which a patient is active and committed to bring a therapeutic results.

The medical non adherence is classified into two classes. The first class refers to the primary non adherence which involves a pharmacist not dispensing the medication prescribed to the patients. The secondary non adherence involves the patient not taking the prescribed medications correctly or missing the doses. Poor medical adherence leads to the poor health outcomes and often leads to patient losing their lives (Piña et al, 2021).
